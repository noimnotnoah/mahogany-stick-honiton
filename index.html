The Mahogany Stick Michael Sharpe — Walking Stick Maker, Honiton

Michael Sharpe has been working wood his whole life. Forty years a carpenter, and now — for the last fifteen — he has spent his days in a garden workshop on the lane behind the old railway line in Honiton, making walking sticks the way they have always been made.

There is no machinery louder than a hand-plane. No glue. No shortcuts. Each stick begins as a branch, cut from a Devon hedge row, and ends — days or weeks later — as something someone will lean on for years. Michael calls it the best work he has ever done, and he means it.

Materials We Use

All wood is local — cut from hedgerows and coppices within a few miles of Honiton, dried in Michael's shed for at least a year, then worked by hand on a shavehorse he built himself.

Hazel

Light and springy, with a beautiful warm bark. Hazel makes the finest walking sticks — supple enough for a full day's ramble, strong enough for a lifetime.

Ash

Tough and straight-grained. Ash is Michael's choice for hiking staffs that take real weight. It ages to a pale silver if left unpeeled, or a rich honey if worked.

Blackthorn

Dense, heavy, and characterful. Blackthorn keeps its knots and twists — no two sticks alike. The bark is dark and craggy; under it lies wood the colour of old ivory.

Where to Buy

Michael sells at the Honiton craft fair through the summer, and at the Christmas market on the High Street. Come and handle the sticks — he will talk you through each one, help you find the right height and weight, and if nothing is quite right he will make you one that is.

Outside of market days, you are welcome at his home workshop by appointment. The gate is usually open — knock on and see. If he is at the shavehorse, he will be glad of the company.
