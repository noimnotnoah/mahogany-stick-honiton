The Mahogany Stick.

Walking sticks made in Honiton.

Michael Sharpe makes walking sticks. He worked as a carpenter for forty years; now he carves sticks from his garden workshop in Honiton, on the lane behind the old railway line.

He uses local wood — hazel, ash, blackthorn — cut by hand, dried in the shed, peeled and shaped on a shavehorse he built himself. Every stick is different. Some are straight and clean, meant for a long day's walk on the South West Coast Path. Others keep a crook or a twist, the wood's own character left in. He also turns hiking staffs, a little heavier, with a brass ferrule and a leather wrist strap.

If you want something particular — a handle carved to your hand, a wrap of stitched leather, a piece of antler from a local deer — he will do that too. It takes longer, but the work is worth it.

Standard sticks £25–£45

Custom handles (leather, antler, carved) £50–£80

Michael sells at the Honiton craft fair, at the annual Christmas market, and from his home by appointment — or just knock on, because the gate is usually open. Fifteen years he has been at it now, and he still calls it the best work he has ever done.
